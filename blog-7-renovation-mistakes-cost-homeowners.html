7 Renovation Mistakes That Cost Homeowners Thousands

The seven decisions we see add thousands to renovation budgets — and how to avoid every one of them.

By the NEVACO BUILD Team · October 10, 2026

A renovation is one of the biggest investments most homeowners make — and also one of the easiest to get wrong. At NEVACO BUILD, we’ve walked into enough projects started by other contractors to know the mistakes below aren’t about picking the “wrong” tile or paint color. They’re the kind of decisions that quietly add thousands of dollars to a project, stretch a 3-month timeline into 8, or leave homeowners with work that has to be redone. Here are the seven we see most often, and how to avoid them.

1.Skipping the Permit Step

It’s tempting to think a permit is just paperwork that slows things down. In reality, unpermitted work can block a home sale, void insurance coverage, and force a homeowner to tear out finished work to bring it up to code later — at a far higher cost than doing it right the first time. Every NEVACO BUILD project is permitted and inspected properly from day one, so it never becomes a problem down the line.

2.Choosing a Contractor Based on Price Alone

The lowest quote is rarely the lowest final cost. A quote that’s dramatically below others usually means something is missing — lower-grade materials, no contingency for what’s behind the walls, or corners that get cut once the project is underway and change orders start piling up. A detailed, honest estimate upfront is worth more than a number that looks good on paper.

3.Not Budgeting a Contingency

Older homes especially tend to hide surprises — outdated wiring, water damage, structural issues — behind walls and floors that can’t be seen until demolition starts. A renovation budget with no cushion for the unexpected almost always runs over, and the shortfall usually surfaces mid-project, when stopping isn’t really an option.

4.Changing the Design Mid-Construction

Every change after construction starts costs more than the same change made on paper — in materials already ordered, labor already scheduled, and work that sometimes has to be undone before the new direction can begin. Locking in the design and finishes before the first wall comes down is one of the simplest ways to protect a budget. It’s why our design & planning phase happens before construction starts, not alongside it.

5.Underestimating the Timeline

Permits, material lead times, inspections, and the sequencing of different trades all add up. A homeowner who assumes a full renovation will take “a couple months” without checking that assumption against the actual scope often ends up living in a construction zone far longer than planned — and making rushed decisions to speed things up, which tends to cost more.

6.Not Getting Everything in Writing

A verbal agreement about scope, materials, or timeline means nothing once a dispute comes up. A detailed written contract — covering scope of work, materials, payment schedule, and timeline — is what protects both sides when memory and expectations don’t line up months later. Every NEVACO BUILD project starts with a clear, written scope, so there’s no ambiguity about what’s included.

7.Prioritizing Trends Over Resale Value

A bold, highly personal design choice can feel exciting during planning and become a liability at resale. The renovations that hold their value tend to balance personal taste with choices a future buyer would also want — something our team talks through with every client before committing to a direction that’s hard to reverse.

Most of these mistakes aren’t about bad luck — they’re about decisions made without the full picture. At NEVACO BUILD, managing permits, planning for contingencies, and locking in design before construction starts isn’t an extra step — it’s how every project is run. Frequently asked questions

What’s the most common renovation mistake homeowners make?

Choosing a contractor based on the lowest price alone, without checking what’s actually included in the quote.

How much contingency should I budget for a renovation?

A common guideline is 10–20% above the base estimate, especially for older homes where unexpected issues behind walls are more likely.

Why does skipping a permit matter if the work still gets done?

Unpermitted work can block a future home sale, void insurance claims, and may need to be torn out and redone to meet code — at a much higher cost than doing it properly the first time.
