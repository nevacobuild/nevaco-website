Does a Bathroom Renovation Add Value to Your Home?

What actually adds resale value — and the specific mistakes that turn a cheap bathroom renovation into an expensive one a few years later.

By the NEVACO BUILD Team · October 15, 2026

A bathroom renovation is one of the highest-return projects in a home — but only when it's done right. Here's what actually adds value, and the specific mistakes NEVACO BUILD sees turn a cheap renovation into an expensive one a few years later.

Does It Actually Add Value?

Bathrooms are one of the first things buyers and appraisers judge a home by. A well-finished bathroom recovers a strong share of its cost at resale — often more than most other rooms — because the upgrade is immediately visible and usable, with none of the "imagine what this could look like" guesswork a kitchen or addition requires.

Mistakes That Lead to Leaks and Mold

1. Rushed or Incomplete Waterproofing

A continuous membrane behind all tiled surfaces is what keeps water out of the subfloor and wall cavities. Skipped or incomplete waterproofing is the single biggest cause of hidden water damage.

2. Exhaust Fan Vented Into the Attic, Not Outside

A fan that dumps moist air into the attic instead of venting it outdoors just moves the mold problem one floor up, where it's even harder to spot.

3. Incorrectly Sloped Shower Drain

A drain that doesn't slope correctly leaves standing water against the membrane every time the shower is used, wearing through a seal that was never meant to hold standing water.

4. No Permit or Inspection on Plumbing/Electrical Work

A permit isn't paperwork — it's a second set of eyes checking the exact things (venting, GFCI protection, drain slope) that cause problems years later if missed. Every NEVACO BUILD bathroom renovation is permitted and inspected as part of the scope, not treated as an optional add-on.

What Gets Cut When a Quote Is Too Low

1. Subfloor Reinforcement

Skipped to save on labor and material; shows up later as a floor that flexes or cracks tile.

2. Full-Height Waterproofing

Membrane applied only where it's visible, not behind the full wet wall; fails exactly where it can't be seen.

3. Proper Ventilation Ductwork

Running duct to the exterior costs more than a fan that just vents into the ceiling cavity.

4. A Detailed Written Scope

Without one, there's no way to know what grade of materials or methods a low price is actually buying.

What a Properly Done Bathroom Renovation Includes

Continuous waterproofing membrane behind all wet areas. Correctly sloped and tested drains. Exhaust ventilation ducted to the exterior. GFCI-protected electrical near water sources. A permit and inspection where the scope requires it. None of this shows up in a photo — it's what determines whether the bathroom still performs the same way in ten years, and it's the baseline every NEVACO BUILD bathroom renovation is built to.

A bathroom renovation can be one of the best investments in a home, or one of the most expensive mistakes — the difference is almost always in the parts you can't see once the tile goes up. Frequently asked questions

Does a bathroom renovation really add value to a home?

Yes — bathrooms recover a strong share of their renovation cost at resale, often more than most other rooms, because the upgrade is immediately visible and usable.

What causes mold in a renovated bathroom?

Most mold in a renovated bathroom traces back to incomplete waterproofing, an exhaust fan vented into the attic instead of outside, or a shower drain that isn't sloped correctly — all of which let moisture sit inside walls or ceilings where it can't dry out.

How do I know if a bathroom renovation quote is skipping something important?

Ask for a detailed written scope that names the waterproofing method, where the exhaust fan vents to, and whether a permit is included for any plumbing or electrical work. A quote that can't answer those specifically is usually leaving one of them out.
