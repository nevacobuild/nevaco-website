Commercial Renovation Costs in the GTA: Budgeting for Office, Retail & Restaurant Buildouts

What actually drives pricing for office, retail, and restaurant renovations — and how to budget against real numbers instead of guesses.

By the NEVACO BUILD Team · October 9, 2026

Commercial renovation costs vary more than residential ones — a quiet professional office, a high-traffic retail store, and a commercial kitchen have very different mechanical, electrical, and finish requirements, even at the same square footage. Here’s what actually drives the number on your quote.

The biggest cost driver: scope, not square footage

Two spaces the same size can cost very differently to renovate depending on what’s involved:

  • Cosmetic refresh — paint, flooring, lighting fixtures, minor millwork — is the lowest-cost tier, with no structural or systems work.
  • Mid-scope tenant improvement — new partition walls, updated electrical and lighting, some mechanical changes — sits in the middle.
  • Full gut renovation — new HVAC, electrical service upgrades, plumbing (critical for restaurants and medical spaces), structural changes, and a full change-of-use permit — is the highest-cost tier, often by a wide margin.

Before you can budget accurately, you need to know which of these your project actually is — and that usually requires a site visit, not a square-footage estimate alone.

What pushes costs higher in a commercial project

  • Mechanical and electrical upgrades — older GTA commercial buildings often need electrical service upgrades or new HVAC zoning to support a new use, especially for restaurants and medical/dental spaces
  • Plumbing for food service — grease traps, additional fixtures, and venting add significant cost to restaurant and café buildouts
  • Fire and life safety systems — sprinkler modifications, fire separation work, and exit changes required by a change of use
  • Accessibility upgrades — AODA-compliant washrooms, entrances, and clearances if the existing space doesn’t already meet current code
  • Landlord requirements — many commercial leases specify finish standards, approved contractor lists, or base-building restoration obligations at lease-end, which affect both scope and cost

Where businesses commonly under-budget

The most common budgeting mistake isn’t the renovation itself — it’s underestimating permit timelines, mechanical/electrical surprises behind existing walls and ceilings in older buildings, and landlord-driven requirements that aren’t obvious until a lease is reviewed in detail. Building in a contingency, and getting a contractor involved before a lease is signed, avoids most of these surprises.

Getting an accurate number

Frequently asked questions

What’s the difference between a cosmetic refresh and a full commercial renovation in terms of cost?

A cosmetic refresh (paint, flooring, fixtures) is the lowest-cost option with no systems work. A full renovation involving new HVAC, electrical, plumbing, and a change of use costs substantially more and takes longer to permit.

Why do restaurant and medical space renovations typically cost more than office renovations?

They require specialized plumbing, ventilation, and fire/life safety systems that standard office space doesn’t need, plus stricter code compliance for their occupancy type.

Can my landlord affect my renovation budget?

Yes. Many commercial leases specify finish standards, approved contractors, or restoration requirements at lease-end — all of which should be reviewed before finalizing a renovation budget.
